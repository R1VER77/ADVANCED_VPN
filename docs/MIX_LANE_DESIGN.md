# MIX_LANE_DESIGN.md — Async mixnet lane + PQ-Sphinx for AVPN/Mesh

Status: design proposal. Complements `MESH_DESIGN.md`; implements the "mixnet lane
for async traffic" already named in the repo's Limits section.

Scope of this document:

1. **Relay buffering design** — turning an existing relay into a store-and-forward
   mix: cell format, delay queues, overflow policy, replay cache, crash and
   failure handling, mailbox retrieval.
2. **PQ-Sphinx header layout** — wire format and byte budgets for replacing the
   nested-WireGuard path construction with Sphinx packets, with ML-KEM per-hop
   key agreement, plus a SURB (single-use reply block) format.

All arithmetic in the tables below is generated by `tools/mix_lane_math.py`
(attached alongside this doc) — regenerate rather than hand-edit.

## 0. What this lane is and is not

| Lane | Latency class | Mechanism | Claim we may make |
|---|---|---|---|
| existing low-latency lanes | interactive | nested WG + per-hop shaping (Maybenot) | per-hop defense raises GPA cost; no mixnet-grade unlinkability |
| **mix lane (this doc)** | async, seconds | Sphinx over buffered mixes + cover | computational unlinkability per mix path, with padding/delay bounds; NOT information-theoretic |
| DC-net control channel (future, mesh mode only) | round-based, 2–10 s | small cells, enrollment/chain-setup events only | computational unlinkability within the cell (upgradable to IT with pre-provisioned pads) |

The mix lane carries: async messages, mail, queued transfers, control events,
SURB replies. It never carries interactive streams — that is the whole point of
keeping the two lanes separate and is what lets the Limits section be sharpened
honestly (see §7).

## Part I — Relay buffering design

### 1.1 Cell format

All cells on the mix lane are a fixed size `S`, chosen per deployment from
{2048, 4096, 8192} bytes. Fixed size is mandatory: cells ride the existing
constant-rate framing, and size must never leak position or content type.

```
+-----------------------+------------------------+-------------------+
| Sphinx header (H)     | payload (P)            | AEAD tag (16 B)   |
+-----------------------+------------------------+-------------------+
|<--------------------------- fixed cell size S -------------------->|
```

- `P = S − H − 16`. H depends on path length and PQ option (Part II, §2.4).
- Payload is client-side AEAD (ChaCha20-Poly1305, 256-bit key derived inside the
  Sphinx key schedule); mixes never see plaintext.
- Recommendation: **S = 4096 with header option E (v = 3)** → P = 2684 B per
  cell, 66% wire efficiency, no fragmentation needed for control events and
  small messages; larger async payloads fragment across cells with a
  client-visible reassembly header inside the encrypted payload.

### 1.2 Mix processing pipeline (per cell)

A mix performs, in constant data-independent time:

1. **Decapsulate / derive** — recover the per-mix shared secret from its header
   slot (§2.3).
2. **Replay check** — lookup tag in the rotating replay cache (§1.6); on hit,
   drop.
3. **Verify MAC** — over the remainder of the header + payload.
4. **Peel** — derive next-hop address and the re-blinding pad for the next
   hop's header slot; overwrite the consumed slot with cover bytes.
5. **Schedule** — enqueue the cell with a delay drawn from the delay
   distribution (§1.3).
6. **Forward** — when the delay timer fires, transmit on the existing
   constant-rate server↔server link.

**Failure discipline (anti-tagging):** steps 2–4 never emit distinguishable
error behavior. A cell that fails MAC or replay is *not dropped immediately*;
it is enqueued into the same delay distribution and then discarded at the
timer. Otherwise an active adversary could tag a cell (flip one payload bit)
and learn from *where* the flow died whether its target position was reached.
Uniform-in-time failure is the only acceptable failure.

### 1.3 Delay distribution

Per-mix delay ~ **Exponential(μ)**, memoryless, which is what decorrelates the
departure process from the arrival process. End-to-end delay for a v-hop path
is Erlang(v, μ). With v = 3:

| per-hop mean | p50 | p95 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 s | 0.2 s | 0.5 s | 0.7 s |
| 0.5 s  | 0.4 s | 1.0 s | 1.4 s |
| 1.0 s  | 0.9 s | 2.1 s | 2.8 s |
| 2.0 s  | 1.8 s | 4.2 s | 5.6 s |

Defaults: μ tuned for **1.0 s mean per hop** (`mix.delay_mean_ms = 1000`),
configurable 0.25–2.0 s. Anonymity scales with the mean; latency budget for an
async lane tolerates the p99 of 2.8 s easily.

### 1.4 Queues and overflow

Two priority classes per mix:

- **payload queue** (client cells): drop-last, counted in RAM-only metrics.
- **cover queue** (loop/drop cover, §1.5): drop-first on pressure.

Bounds from Little's law (steady occupancy ≈ arrival rate × mean delay), with
4× headroom as `Q_max` (4 KB cells):

| cells/s | mean delay | steady E[Q] | Q_max (4×) | RAM @ Q_max |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 0.5 s | 50 | 200 | 1 MB |
| 100 | 1.0 s | 100 | 400 | 2 MB |
| 500 | 1.0 s | 500 | 2000 | 8 MB |
| 1280 | 1.0 s | 1280 | 5120 | 20 MB |
| 1280 | 2.0 s | 2560 | 10240 | 40 MB |

A hobbyist mix relay on a free-tier VM (≈1 GB RAM) can hold `Q_max = 8192`
cells (32 MB) comfortably; that sustains ~2000 cells/s at 1 s mean delay.
Overflow order: drop cover first, then oldest payload; increment an in-RAM
counter only (never logged to disk — the no-persistence rule applies to
metrics too).

No backpressure signaling hop-to-hop: backpressure messages would themselves
be a timing side channel. Overload shows up as cover drops and is visible to
the chain monitor via gossip liveness, not per-flow.

### 1.5 Cover traffic (reuses the rate classes)

- **Client → mix (loop cover):** Poisson `λ_D`; the client sends cover to
  itself around the full path whenever idle, so "user is active" is not
  observable at the entry.
- **Mix → mix (link cover):** constant-rate framing on server↔server links
  already emits continuously; a fraction of those cells are loop cover
  terminating at the receiving mix (`λ_M`), the rest are the real constant-rate
  stream. No change to the existing framing — cover is simply new cell
  *content*.
- **Payload pacing:** when a real message is sent it displaces cover within
  the same constant-rate envelope (the "one-mode rule" extended to cells).

Illustrative 24/7 volume per rate class (label these as illustrative in
config docs; actual class bitrates are deployment-specific):

| class rate | GB/month | cells/s (4 KB) |
|---|---|---|
| 64 kbit/s | 20.7 | 2.0 |
| 256 kbit/s | 82.9 | 7.8 |
| 1 Mbit/s | 331.8 | 31.2 |
| 4 Mbit/s | 1327.1 | 125.0 |

Even `metered` (64 kbit/s) sustains ~2 cells/s, enough for loop cover on an
idle path. Cover overhead is the price of the anonymity claim; rate classes
are how users choose what to pay.

### 1.6 Replay protection

Per-mix, RAM-only:

- **Filter:** 2^20-slot cuckoo/rotating Bloom filter over per-cell replay tags
  (derived from the header MAC), with a time window `T_replay = 4 × p99
  path delay` (≈ 12 s at defaults; use 60 s to be safe).
- **Rotation:** two generations (current + previous) XOR-swapped on
  `T_replay`, so a tag is remembered for between `T_replay` and `2×T_replay`.
- **Restart:** cache is empty after crash — accepted, documented risk: replayed
  cells survive only if injected after a mid-path restart *and* before the
  client's mailbox-level dedup sees both copies. Client nonce dedup (§1.8)
  is the backstop, because the mailbox decrypts and can compare.

### 1.7 Failure handling

**Mix crash (RAM lost):** in-flight cells die with the process — by design,
no forensic queue, no disk state. No hop-to-hop retransmit exists or should
exist (retransmits would create re-identifiable flows). Recovery is
end-to-end:

- Client retransmit timer `RTO = SRTT + 4·RTTVar` measured to the mailbox,
  capped at 3 attempts, exponential backoff. Each attempt uses a **fresh
  path and a fresh SURB** — never reuse a SURB.
- Path liveness: the chain monitor (already implemented as self-healing
  chains) notices a dead mix via gossip and the chain builder reroutes
  subsequent cells; in-flight cells are simply lost and rely on retransmit.

**Dead destination / mailbox unreachable:** cells time out in the mailbox's
own bounded store (see below) and are dropped. Senders detect via reply
timeout, same as any async system.

**Mailbox store:** bounded (e.g., 256 MB), FIFO-with-TTL (e.g., 24 h), RAM
only. Retrieval is anonymous pull (§1.8). When full, oldest first. The
mailbox cannot read payloads (client AEAD); it only sees a mailbox tag and a
SURB-less pull credential.

**Tagging / active adversary:** covered by §1.2 failure discipline plus
payload AEAD at the endpoint. A tagged cell either survives to the mailbox
and fails client-side decryption (indistinguishable from cover junk) or dies
mid-path with uniform timing. The adversary learns nothing about *where* the
tag took effect.

### 1.8 Mailbox retrieval protocol

The mesh's existing blind-signed credits (unlinkable issue/spend) are the
natural retrieval credential:

1. Client builds a **pull token**: blind-signed credit spent to a mailbox
   epoch number (rotating, e.g., hourly) — the signature is unlinkable to
   issuance, so the mailbox cannot connect pull sessions to identity.
2. Pull request travels over a fresh Sphinx path to the mailbox: "return
   everything addressed to tag X".
3. Mailbox returns the (AEAD-encrypted) cells to the *reply* path embedded
   in each stored message's SURB — or, for a pull-based variant, in the
   encrypted response on the same path. SURB-based reply is preferred: it
   exercises the same machinery the return traffic needs anyway.
4. Client decrypts, dedups by 128-bit nonce inside the plaintext, presents
   to the application.

Mailbox operators are exit-role-adjacent but see *less* than exits: no
destination addresses, only inbound Sphinx cells.

### 1.9 What the mix node does NOT get

- No view of path length, position, or next-next hop (Sphinx property).
- No persistent state of any kind (queues, replay cache, metrics: RAM only).
- No ability to link a cell's entry to its exit beyond what timing analysis
  of *its own* two links allows — which is precisely what §1.3's delay
  distribution and §1.5's cover exist to blunt.

## Part II — PQ-Sphinx header layout

### 2.1 Why Sphinx instead of nested WG for the mix lane

The low-latency lanes keep nested WireGuard — the client must know the path
there, and that is acceptable per-hop-defended. The mix lane needs properties
nested tunnels cannot give:

- **Per-mix cryptographic unlinkability:** a mix provably cannot tell its hop
  index or the path before/after it.
- **SURBs:** anonymous return paths — no way to do this with nested tunnels
  short of pre-building reverse tunnels, which are linkable.
- **Tagging resistance:** MAC failure at hop i does not reveal i (with the
  §1.2 discipline).
- **Fixed-size, replay-resistant packets** that match constant-rate framing.

### 2.2 Header wire format

```
Header := fixed(4) || per_hop[v]  where
fixed  := version(1) || n_hops(1) || flags(1) || reserved(1)
per_hop[i] := kem_ct[i] || mac[i](32) || routing_block[i](48)

Cell := Header || payload || tag(16)
```

- `kem_ct[i]`: the key-agreement ciphertext hop i consumes (32 B X25519
  element, or ML-KEM-512 768 B, or ML-KEM-768 1088 B — options below).
- `mac[i]`: 32 B, BLAKE3 keyed from the hop-i derived key, covers the
  remaining header suffix + payload.
- `routing_block[i]`: 48 B symmetric ciphertext (decoded by hop i only):
  `{ next_hop (16 B truncated member ID) || transport hint (8 B) ||
  commands (8 B) || pad (16 B) }`. Commands include: FORWARD, TERMINATE_AT_
  MAILBOX, EMBED_SURB, plus spare bits for future congestion-free signals.

Routing blocks for hops after i are re-encrypted with hop i's derived key
(classical Sphinx slot shifting), so each mix decodes exactly one block.

### 2.3 PQ key agreement and re-blinding

Classical Sphinx blinds public keys multiplicatively per hop; ML-KEM does not
compose that way, so use **XOR re-blinding of ciphertexts**:

1. Client encapsulates to every hop's *published* ML-KEM public key
   (distributed in the gossip node descriptors, ML-DSA-65 signed — both
   already exist in the mesh design), obtaining shared secrets
   `K_1..K_v` and ciphertexts `ct_1..ct_v`.
2. For i > 1, the client pre-computes `pad_i = HKDF(K_{i-1},
   "reblind" || i)`, XORs it into `ct_i`, and hop i−1 re-XORs the same pad
   (it knows K_{i-1}) while peeling. Hop i strips the pad, then decapsulates.
3. Each `kem_ct` slot is overwritten with cover bytes when consumed, so
   header length never reveals the remaining hop count to a later mix.

Property: hop i sees a fresh-looking, never-before-published `ct_i`, and no
other network observer can match it to anything. **Caveat, stated plainly:**
this KEM re-blinding construction is not a standard, audited construction —
it is the single part of this design that most needs external cryptographic
review before any security claim is made from it. (Known PQ-Sphinx attempts
exist in the literature; none is production-audited, so treat this as
review-pending.)

### 2.4 Byte budgets

Component sizes: X25519 element 32 B; ML-KEM-512 ct 768 B; ML-KEM-768 ct
1088 B (FIPS 203); MAC 32 B; routing block 48 B; fixed 4 B; AEAD tag 16 B.

**Header sizes (bytes):**

| option | v=3 | v=4 | v=5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A classical X25519 only | 340 | 452 | 564 |
| B hybrid X25519 + ML-KEM-512 per hop | 2644 | 3524 | 4404 |
| C ML-KEM-512 only | 2548 | 3396 | 4244 |
| D ML-KEM-768 only | 3508 | 4676 | 5844 |
| **E entry ML-KEM-768 + inner X25519** | **1396** | **1508** | **1620** |

**Payload per cell (v=3):**

| option | S=2048 | S=4096 | S=8192 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A classical | 1692 (83%) | 3740 (91%) | 7836 (96%) |
| B hybrid | 1436 (35%) | 5532 (68%) | — |
| C ML-KEM-512 | 1532 (37%) | 5628 (69%) | — |
| D ML-KEM-768 | 572 (14%) | 4668 (57%) | — |
| **E entry-hybrid** | 636 (31%) | **2684 (66%)** | **6780 (83%)** |

Key observations from the tables:

- Options B/C/D cost a full KEM ciphertext *per hop* — D at 4 KB cells
  leaves only 572 B of payload (14%): unusable.
- **Option E's marginal cost per additional hop is only 112 B** (one more
  X25519 element + MAC + routing block), so 5-hop paths stay cheap (1620 B).
  Longer paths for higher-value traffic become affordable, which is a
  genuinely useful property unique to E.
- ML-KEM CPU cost at the mix is negligible: ML-KEM-768 decapsulation
  ≈ 30 µs/cell on a modern x86 core → ~3.8% of one core at 1280 cells/s,
  15% at 5000 cells/s. The bottleneck is RAM/queue, not crypto.

**Recommendation: option E, S = 4096, v = 3 default / v = 5 optional.**
This mirrors the existing AVPN pattern (PQ at the trust boundary — the entry
is where long-term identity linking matters most) while keeping inner hops
cheap. Trade-off to state in the docs: inner-hop path cryptography remains
classical; an adversary who can break X25519 blinding AND observes multiple
hops could link inner path segments. Against harvest-now-decrypt-later of
*traffic content* the design is still PQ-safe end-to-end, because payload
and per-hop MAC keys derive from the entry ML-KEM secret plus X25519 —
actually, to make that claim cleanly, derive the payload key as
`HKDF(K_mlkem_entry || K_x25519_all_hops)`; then content confidentiality
requires breaking both. State the claim exactly that narrowly.

### 2.5 SURB format

```
SURB := Header(v' hops, reply path) || reply_payload_key_hint(32)
```

- Same wire format as a forward header; the *recipient* of a SURB becomes
  the sender of the reply and learns nothing about who will receive it.
- A SURB is single-use: the client embeds a one-time reply key; the reply
  payload is AEAD'd under `HKDF(reply_key_hint || K_last)` where K_last is
  derived when the SURB's terminal hop is reached.
- Size: with option E, v'=3, a SURB costs 1396 + 32 = 1428 B — a client
  can embed ~2 SURBs per 4096-cell alongside ~1.2 KB of message, or hand
  them out-of-band for long-lived correspondents.
- **Never reuse, never replay:** SURB replay is handled by the same mix
  replay cache (tags derive from header MACs, which are fresh per use).

### 2.6 Key schedule (summary)

```
K_i            : per-hop shared secret (ML-KEM entry / X25519 inner)
hop_mac_key_i  = HKDF(K_i, "mac"  || i)
reblind_pad_i  = HKDF(K_i, "pad"  || i+1)          (entry hop only, §2.3)
routing_key_i  = HKDF(K_i, "rt"   || i)
payload_key    = HKDF(K_1 || K_2 || ... || K_v, "payload")
```

`payload_key` mixes every hop's secret: any single honest mix on the path
contributes entropy the adversary does not hold.

## 3. Integration with existing AVPN/Mesh components

| Existing piece | Role in the mix lane |
|---|---|
| Constant-rate framing + rate classes | cell transport + cover volume control (§1.5) |
| Gossip node descriptors | distribution of mix ML-KEM/ML-DSA keys, mix liveness |
| Diversity chain builder | mix-path selection (distinct operator/provider/country per hop) — unchanged |
| Blind-signed credits | anonymous mailbox pull tokens (§1.8) |
| Censorship ladder (QUIC/443) | carries cells identically to current packets |
| Self-healing chains | dead-mix reroute for subsequent cells |
| Hardware-anchored identity | unchanged; enrollment stays out of the mix lane |

## 4. Implementation order

1. Cell framing + fixed-size pipeline on the relay (no crypto change yet):
   bounded queues, delay distribution, failure discipline. Testable with
   plain nested-WG content in cells.
2. Sphinx header with option A (classical) end-to-end, plus SURBs and
   mailbox — validates the whole design before PQ.
3. Swap to header option E (§2.3 re-blinding) and derive the payload key
   per §2.6.
4. Client retransmit + mailbox pull with blind credits.
5. Only then: DC-net control channel (separate doc), small cells,
   mesh-mode-only.

## 5. Test plan (minimum bar before any claim)

- Uniformity: distributions of inter-departure times at exit vs
  inter-arrival at entry, KS-test, under load and idle (cover on/off —
  the "off" case must fail the test, proving cover does the work).
- Tagging: bit-flip fault injection at every hop position; observable must
  be identical for all positions.
- Replay: replays within/after cache window, post-crash replays.
- Overflow: cover-first drop order verified under sustained overload.
- WF harness reuse: run the existing website-fingerprinting simulation
  against the mix lane; expect the 3.3% closed-world figure to be reported
  *with* its scope (closed-world, lab conditions).

## 6. Honest limits of this design (extend the repo Limits section)

- Mix-lane unlinkability is **computational**, dependent on the §2.3
  re-blinding construction (review-pending) and on Sphinx assumptions.
- Anonymity set = concurrent active mix-lane users per observation window;
  a 15-member mesh is a beautifully engineered anonymity set of 15. Growth
  is a security parameter, not a growth metric.
- A global *active* adversary (selective drop to confirm paths) is not
  fully mitigated; delay distributions and cover raise the cost only.
- Endpoints remain the weakest link: browser, DNS inside the payload,
  behavioral patterns. No protocol fixes those.

## 7. Resulting Limits-section wording (proposed)

> The mix lane provides computational unlinkability with measurable delay
> and padding bounds for asynchronous traffic; it does not provide
> information-theoretic anonymity. The low-latency lanes retain per-hop
> defense only. A global passive adversary remains out of scope for the
> low-latency lanes; for the mix lane the adversary's advantage degrades
> with cover rate, delay mean, and active-user count — parameters we
> publish per release, not claims we make.
