# Technical Comparison: ADVANCED_VPN v0.9.1 vs Mullvad and IVPN

This document compares **ADVANCED_VPN v0.9.1** against the two strongest privacy-focused commercial VPNs (**Mullvad** and **IVPN**) across cryptography, traffic-analysis resistance, censorship circumvention, DNS privacy, sovereignty, and operational limits. Every claim below regarding ADVANCED_VPN is backed by an executable test or a committed measurement in [`docs/evidence/`](evidence/).

## 2. Deep dive: Traffic-analysis defense & the constant-rate stream

Commercial VPNs face a fundamental tension between **bandwidth overhead** and **website-fingerprinting (WF) resistance**:

1. **Undefended tunnels (IVPN, standard WireGuard)** forward packets immediately and preserve packet lengths up to MTU boundaries. A passive ISP observer running a standard `k-FP` + `CUMUL` random-forest classifier identifies pages well above chance (`27–81%` on 30 sites in our lab; `74–82%` on 8 sites).
2. **Event-driven DAITA (Mullvad DAITA v2)** runs Maybenot machines that inject padding bursts and short blocking delays around real traffic bursts. While much stronger than undefended WireGuard, event-driven machines still vary their packet rate between idle periods and active downloads, leaking total transfer volume and burst boundaries.
3. **ADVANCED_VPN v0.9.1 constant-rate stream (`src/daita.rs`)** eliminates the distinction between idle and active tunnel states on the wire:
   - Two Maybenot machines (`stream_grid` + self-sustaining `cover`) run under GotaTun's strict-blocking scheduler (`strict: true`, `Replace: true`).
   - Every clock tick on every staggered slot either releases one queued real WireGuard packet (padded to 1380 bytes inside the 1420-byte cloak cell) or emits a 1380-byte decoy packet.
   - Across `metered` (50 pps/dir), `standard` (500 pps/dir), `fast` (4,000 pps/dir), and `line` (16,000 pps/dir), `tools/throughput_bench.py --shape-idle 5` verifies in CI that the wire packet rate stays constant between an idle tunnel and a saturated bulk download (`docs/evidence/RATE_CLASSES.json`).
   - Against the 30-site `k-FP` + `CUMUL` classifier (`tools/daita_lab.py --sites 30 --loads 10`, chance = `3.3%`), a fixed 6-second window on the camouflaged carrier scores **3.3% on `metered`** and **1.7% on `fast`** (both at or below random guessing, versus `27.0%` and `74.7%` on the unshaped inner link) (`docs/evidence/DAITA_LAB.json`).

## 3. Deep dive: Censorship ladder & active-probing resistance

Censors block VPNs in two ways: **passive DPI + UDP blocking** (dropping all QUIC/UDP 443 or WireGuard handshakes) and **active probing** (connecting to suspect IP:443 endpoints with an HTTP/1.1 or HTTP/3 client to check whether a real web origin answers).

- **Primary QUIC/HTTP/3 cloak (`UDP 443`)**: Every WireGuard packet travels as a constant-size 1420-byte QUIC `DATAGRAM` frame inside a `TLS 1.3 X25519MLKEM768` session offering ALPN `h3`. `nDPI` classifies the flow as `QUIC, category Web`.
- **Automatic TCP/TLS 1.3 fallback (`TCP 443`)**: If UDP 443 cannot complete a handshake within `2 seconds` (`QUIC_FALLBACK_TIMEOUT`), `advanced-vpn-cloak` automatically connects over `TCP 443` using `TLS 1.3` (`ALPN http/1.1`), sends an HTTP `GET /` request carrying the TLS 1.3 exporter proof (`EXPORTER-advanced-vpn-cloak`) in `If-None-Match`, and switches the stream into constant 1420-byte cells (`TCP_CELL_LEN`). Every `10 seconds` (`QUIC_PROBE_INTERVAL`), the client probes UDP 443 in the background and seamlessly upgrades back to QUIC as soon as UDP recovers.
- **Realistic HTML5 decoy origin**: Any client that connects over HTTP/3 (`UDP 443`) or HTTPS (`TCP 443`) without a valid TLS exporter proof receives a standard `200 OK` response (`Content-Type: text/html; charset=utf-8`, `Alt-Svc: h3=":443"; ma=86400`, `Cache-Control: public, max-age=300`) serving a realistic static HTML5 edge status page (`src/cloak.rs`). The VPN carrier is never exposed to unauthenticated peers.

## 4. Honest gaps, active roadmap, and architectural trajectory

A credible security engineering comparison must state clearly where commercial providers currently lead and how `AVPN/Mesh` closes each gap:

1. **Platform coverage & GUI**: ADVANCED_VPN targets **Linux (`x86_64`)** with a rich terminal UI (`ratatui`) and CLI (`avpn`). Mullvad and IVPN ship native graphical clients for Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, and Android.
2. **Independent audit (not yet)**: Mullvad and IVPN undergo periodic external audits (Cure53, Assured, Radically Open Security) and have years of incident history. ADVANCED_VPN has none of that yet: what it has is open source, strict Rust/Python lint gates, mutation-checked tests, and namespace/VM labs in CI (`verify.yml`). That is not a substitute for an independent audit. The audit scope, in order of risk: the paced-link cell crypto and replay window (`src/link.rs`), the cloak's exporter proof and TCP fallback (`src/cloak.rs`), the vendored GotaTun strict-shaping changes, enrollment and KEM sealing (`tools/managed_profile.py`, `tools/keyguard.py`), the blind-RSA credits (`tools/mesh_tokens.py`), the oblivious control plane (`tools/ohttp.py`), and descriptor/gossip parsing. Before any paid review, the custom cryptography is to be proved by software: Tamarin and ProVerif models of the paced-link cells, the cloak's proof, the hybrid KEM sealing, the oblivious control plane, the blind-RSA credits and signed enrollment, each tied to the code by test vectors and Kani harnesses and run in CI (plan: [VERIFICATION_PLAN.md](VERIFICATION_PLAN.md); nothing there is proved yet). Coverage-guided fuzzing (cargo-fuzz) of the cell, cloak and descriptor parsers runs alongside.
3. **From Sovereign Single-Tenant Isolation to an FCMP++-Style Full-Mesh Anonymity Set**: Single-operator mode is a stepping stone, not the final privacy ceiling. While Tor provides circuit-level anonymity over low-cost unpadded links and commercial VPNs pool users under one company's exit IP, the target architecture of **`AVPN/Mesh`** is a **full-mesh anonymity set inspired by FCMP++ (Full-Chain Membership Proofs)**:
   - With multi-member relay fan-in (`members[]` peer tables on every hop), constant-rate bidirectional cover streams ticking continuously on every client↔entry and inter-relay link (`cover: "always-on"`), and unlinkable blind-signed / zero-knowledge membership proofs, an observer watching any exit or inter-hop link sees uniform constant-rate streams from all enrolled mesh participants simultaneously.
   - In that full-mesh regime, any egress connection is equally likely to have originated from **any active participant in the entire mesh** (`1 / N` across the full participant set), rather than being attributable to a single tenant or narrowed to a timing-correlated burst.
4. **Multi-Browser TLS/QUIC Fingerprint Matching (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Brave)**: `advanced-vpn-cloak` currently speaks real HTTP/3 (`quinn`/`rustls`) and HTTP/1.1 TLS 1.3 (`tokio-rustls`) and serves a `200 OK` HTML5 origin using standard `rustls` extension ordering. Because a censor or DPI box that correlates OS/browser traffic can flag a user whose applications use Firefox or Safari while their tunnel emits a static Chrome-only or `rustls`-only fingerprint, the cloak roadmap adds **multi-profile uTLS/QUIC ClientHello and transport-parameter mimicry** (`chrome`, `firefox`, `safari`, `brave`, or `auto` matching the host's default browser) so the outer TLS 1.3 / QUIC handshake matches the exact extension permutation, GREASE values, and ALPN/transport parameters of the browser actually in use.
5. **Per-hop constant-rate shaping (landed in v0.8.0)**: every server↔server link now carries the constant-rate stream (`src/link.rs`). v0.7.0's documents claimed inter-relay cover had landed; it had not (the carrier only ran client↔entry), and v0.8.0 measured that before fixing it (`RATE_CLASSES.json`, `inner_link.before_v0_7_0`).
6. **Mesh Public-Pool VOPRF Verifier**: `AVPN/Mesh` enforces unlinkable Chaum blind RSA-FDH credits in the Python control plane (`tools/mesh_tokens.py`), while the Rust data-plane verifier (`src/mesh/tokens.rs`) remains fail-closed for public-pool spends until a reviewed Rust VOPRF / ZK membership verifier is vendored.
7. **I2P front and mix lane (landed in v0.9.0).** A constant-rate stream keeps packet order, so a global observer of both ends can still correlate flows. v0.9.0 adds the **mix lane** (`avpn mix`: Katzenpost, unmodified, run as separate processes inside the protected namespace; Poisson delays, loop and drop cover, Sphinx packets; seconds of latency, for mail, chat and small fetches). Its Sphinx is post-quantum at every hop (X-Wing). With live websites, a global passive adversary named the sender of 88.2% of requests on a low-latency path and 10.7% on the mix lane, against 10% chance ([MIX_LANE.json](evidence/MIX_LANE.json)). And the **I2P front** (`avpn init --front i2p`): your entry and its cloud provider see I2P routers, never your address, and only the I2P router may leave your device ([I2P_FRONT.json](evidence/I2P_FRONT.json)). Neither Mullvad nor IVPN offers either.
8. **Post-quantum credits (roadmap).** Every layer of our own tunnel is post-quantum (an I2P front's transport is I2P's own classical cryptography, with our post-quantum layers inside it), but the mesh's contribution credits are Chaum blind RSA (`tools/mesh_tokens.py`): an adversary with a quantum computer could forge them without breaking any tunnel. They move to a lattice-based blind signature before this project calls itself post-quantum throughout ([VERIFICATION_PLAN.md](VERIFICATION_PLAN.md)).
9. **A smaller post-quantum mix lane (in evaluation).** Option E (entry ML-KEM-768, inner X25519) cuts the Sphinx header from X-Wing's 7,164 bytes to 1,396. It is built on audited primitives and byte-checked against an independent reference (`crates/avpn-mix-lane`). It replaces Katzenpost only after its cutover gates pass, including an external review of its one non-standard construction ([DESIGN_PACK_ASSESSMENT.md](DESIGN_PACK_ASSESSMENT.md)).
10. **Verifiable RAM-only relays and a MASQUE front (roadmap).** Attestation statements in relay descriptors (TPM2 or SEV-SNP quotes) would make "RAM-only" checkable instead of claimed. A MASQUE (CONNECT-UDP) front would make the camouflage real HTTP/3 rather than HTTP/3-shaped.
